Rankzap, five ways

Five competing directions for the voter's three most important moments: arriving, ranking, and watching the count. Each phone below is a live mockup with the same poll and the same ballots: drag to rank, switch the counting method, and watch new votes arrive. Open a direction for its palette, type and motion notes.

1 · Ballot Paper

A printed ballot. Number the boxes in pen, get stamped.

2 · Podium

Loud and flat. Higher places are physically bigger.

3 · Election Night

A live broadcast. The count is the show.

4 · Tiles

Calm and tactile. Rank only what you care about.

5 · Head to Head

Pick one of two; your ranking builds itself.

How they compare

DirectionStrongest atProsCons
Ballot PaperNewsreader · Hanken · Caveat Trust and meaning. Makes "ranked choice" self-explanatory.
  • Most ownable concept: the room code becomes a ballot serial.
  • Tap-to-number supports partial ballots naturally.
  • Stamp and red-pen circle give closing moments real ceremony.
  • Skeuomorphic; can feel quaint for casual "what's for lunch" polls.
  • Three typefaces; handwriting face needs care at small sizes and in other scripts.
  • Dark mode is awkward for a paper metaphor.
PodiumArchivo (width axis) Energy and legibility from across a room. Fits the "zap" in the name.
  • Instantly recognisable; the staircase explains ranking without words.
  • One family, few colours: cheap to build and theme.
  • Great for parties, classrooms, shared screens.
  • Condensed caps break down with long options (the app allows 200 chars).
  • Yellow everywhere tires quickly; needs a calmer admin view.
  • Variable row heights make dragging harder to aim on long lists.
Election NightBarlow Condensed · Barlow The live results moment, especially projected for a group.
  • Makes realtime visible: deltas, crowd presence, crawl.
  • Points-per-slot on the ballot teaches the counting method.
  • Natural "present mode" for the admin.
  • Dark-only identity; the app currently auto-switches themes.
  • Busy; the ticker and pulses need restraint and reduced-motion care.
  • The ballot is the least novel of the five.
TilesGeist Everyday usability on a phone, long lists, long option text.
  • Tray and pile solve the 15-option problem without changing the data model.
  • Calm system that extends easily to admin, settings, dark mode.
  • Best drag feel: lift, sink, springs.
  • Least distinctive; at risk of reading as "nice generic app".
  • Personality has to come from motion and copy rather than visuals.
Head to HeadInstrument Serif · Instrument Sans Getting honest rankings out of people who hate ranking.
  • Genuinely new interaction; no dragging needed at all.
  • The matchup grid is an honest picture of Copeland.
  • Shines on polls with many options.
  • More taps for short lists (6 options takes about 10 questions).
  • Needs a drag fallback and an "edit my order" step anyway.
  • The matrix is hard to read past 8 options.

Recommendation: Tiles as the base, with Election Night for the results stage.

Rankzap is used on phones, by people who've just been sent a link, with options up to 200 characters long and automatic light and dark themes. Tiles is the only direction that holds up under all of that without compromise. Its tray and pile also fixes the hardest real problem, long lists, using the "unranked counts as 0" rule the tally already has.

Tiles alone is too quiet, so borrow the strongest ideas from the others:

  1. Election Night's results as a big-screen "present" mode for the admin: score deltas, crowd presence, method switcher.
  2. Points per slot from Election Night on the ballot, so voters see what Borda or Dowdall does with their order.
  3. Head to Head as an optional ballot mode, offered automatically when a poll has more than about 8 options.
  4. From Podium, scaling the rank number by place and springy numeral punches, applied lightly.

If you'd rather the brand be the headline, Podium is the most memorable alternative. It would need a calmer admin and a long-text fallback.
